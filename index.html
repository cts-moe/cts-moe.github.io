Abstract

Perceptive legged locomotion across discontinuous terrain requires shared locomotion knowledge, task-specific maneuvers, and smooth transitions between them. Existing approaches train specialized policies independently and either switch among them with a hierarchical selector or distill them into a monolithic policy. Both fix the constituent behaviors before deployment, and both are assessed through aggregate course outcomes that leave behavior at terrain boundaries uncharacterized. We instead formulate multi-terrain locomotion as multi-task reinforcement learning and jointly train a perception-gated dense mixture-of-experts actor with task-specific critics: soft routing composes expert knowledge into continuously varying behavior, while separated critics isolate heterogeneous objectives so that specialization survives joint optimization. A concurrent teacher–student formulation transfers this composition from privileged terrain information to onboard depth, so that at deployment routing is inferred from perception alone, with no terrain label, selector, or distillation stage. Against hierarchical and DAgger-distilled baselines, the policy raises connected-course success to 93.3% from 73.3% and 53.3%, lowers mean action jerk by about a third across the course, and avoids the switching transient that hard selection introduces at boundaries. On a Unitree Go1, it completes an extended outdoor course in 10 of 10 trials and transfers to four outdoor terrain types.

Method Contributions
  1. An asymmetric multi-task formulation in which task-specific rewards and critics isolate heterogeneous returns, so a single shared actor learns specialized behaviors jointly.
  2. A perception-conditioned MoE router, transferred to onboard depth, that composes these behaviors without a terrain label, selector, or distillation stage.
  3. A transition-centric evaluation against hierarchical and distilled baselines retrained under a matched protocol, measuring success on a connected course with held-out segments and switch-aligned action jerk, followed by hardware deployment on a Unitree Go1.

Over 15 trials at 0.5 m/s with a pure-pursuit controller, success requires completing every segment.

Success Rate by Terrain

Among deployable policies, CTS-MoE attains the best or tied success on every terrain. On unseen discrete terrain it reaches 73.3%, above 53.3% for the best deployable baseline, 20.0% for the hierarchical oracle, and 11.1% for the distilled policy. The mixture only helps with perception: over the six training terrains it is detrimental for blind policies (77.8% vs 83.0%) and beneficial for perceptive ones (96.3% vs 88.5%).

Expert Composition

Each terrain exhibits a distinct but overlapping expert pattern, suggesting that terrain-dependent behavior emerges through compositions of shared experts rather than a one-to-one assignment. Off-diagonal cosine similarities range from 0.48 to 0.73, indicating shared action structure alongside substantial differences. Behavior is carried by the learned routing: replacing the router outputs with uniform weights at inference drops success to 2.2%.
